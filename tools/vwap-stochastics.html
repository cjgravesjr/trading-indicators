VWAP and Stochastics

The math, then a simulated SPX session you can scrub bar by bar. Make your call before you move the slider.

1 · VWAP: the volume-weighted mean price since the open

Definition

Anchor at the session open, t = 0 (6:30 PT). With price p(τ) and volume rate v(τ):

VWAP(t) = ∫0t p(τ) v(τ) dτ∫0t v(τ) dτ = N(t)V(t)

On bars, each bar's price is its typical price pi = (Hi + Li + Ci)/3:

VWAPn = Σi≤n pi viΣi≤n vi

It's the first moment of the day's price distribution, weighted by volume: the average price every share traded at today.

It's a low-pass filter whose capacitor keeps growing

Differentiate with the quotient rule (N′ = pv, V′ = v):

dVWAPdt = v(t)V(t) · ( p(t) − VWAP(t) )

That's an RC circuit, dy/dt = (x − y)/τ, with a time constant that changes:

τeff(t) = V(t) / v(t)

Early in the day V is small, so VWAP chases price. By noon V is huge and VWAP barely moves. A spike in v (news, the open) shrinks τ for a moment and pulls VWAP hard.

Bands: volume-weighted standard deviation

σ2n = Σ vi (pi − VWAPn)2Σ vi = Σ vi pi2Σ vi − VWAPn2

The second form needs only three running sums (Σv, Σvp, Σvp²), which is how indicators compute it in O(1) per bar. The z-score z = (p − VWAP)/σ tells you how stretched price is.

How to use it in your system

  • Driver: holding above VWAP after 6:45 = Up, holding below = Down, crossing = Mixed. It's one of the six drivers on your Game Plan.
  • Trend vs chop: trend days rarely cross it (your 12:45 score uses fewer than 3 crosses). Chop days cross it again and again.
  • Pullback entries: on a trend day, a pullback to VWAP or the ±1σ band that holds is a Leg 2 entry spot.
  • Stretch: beyond ±2σ the move is extended. That's a take-profit zone, not an entry, unless a sweep and change of character print there.
  • Why it works: institutions benchmark their fills against VWAP. Big buyers who need to get filled at or below it keep defending it on up days.

2 · Stochastics: where the close sits in the recent range

Definition

Over the last n bars (14 by default), with highest high Hn and lowest low Ln:

%K = 100 · C − LnHn − Ln ,   %D = SMA3(%K)

It's min-max normalization of the close onto [0, 100]. "Slow" stochastics smooth %K with another 3-bar average first. Williams %R is the same quantity shifted: %R = %K − 100.

What it really measures

Not speed, position. %K = 90 means the close is in the top 10% of the last n bars' range. It's bounded and scale-free, so it reads the same on SPX and on a $5 stock.

Edge cases worth knowing: when Hn − Ln is tiny (dead chop), small ticks swing it 0 → 100. And it jumps when an old extreme drops out of the window, even if price didn't move.

How to use it in your system

  • In a trend it pins. On a down day %K sits under 20 for a long time. "Oversold" there is not a buy signal. Fading it is the same mistake as fading the open.
  • Entry timing with the trend: on a down day, wait for %K to bounce above 50–80 on a pullback, then cross back down under %D. That's the pullback ending and Leg 2 starting.
  • Reversals: divergence (new price low, higher %K low) at a data level plus a sweep and change of character. Stochastics alone never confirms a reversal.
